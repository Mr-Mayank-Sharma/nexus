# HotWax Marketing & Partner Strategy — Reverse-Engineered

> How HotWax markets itself, positions its product, and works its partner channel — extracted from the **NRF Session Strategy** and **HotWax x Tuihq** meetings. This is the marketing playbook Nexus should study, then beat.

---

## 1. The Core Marketing Insight

**HotWax markets an industry problem, not a product.** The NRF session strategy is explicit: the presentation must "address the tension between store and e-commerce teams and position a shared scoreboard as the path to aligned performance" — and it must "focus on the industry problem and customer outcomes rather than become a product pitch." HotWax's marketing is **thought-leadership-led**: they win attention by naming a real operational problem, then position their OMS as the natural solution.

**Lesson for Nexus:** Lead with the problem ("store and e-commerce teams are fighting over the same inventory"), not the feature list. The product is the answer to a story, not the story itself.

## 2. The NRF Conference Strategy (the flagship marketing play)

### 2.1 The Positioning Concept: "The Shared Scoreboard"

The central narrative HotWax is building for NRF:

- **The problem:** Store teams and e-commerce teams are measured on **different, conflicting goals** (store revenue vs. online conversion), so they fight over inventory and undercut each other.
- **The concept:** A **shared scoreboard** — one set of metrics both teams are measured against — aligns them.
- **The proof:** HotWax dashboards use **retailer-specific attribution strategies** and **order-management data** to show both teams the same truth.
- **The framing rule:** Present the industry problem and customer outcomes. **Do NOT turn it into a product demo.** HotWax is "a relevant solution to a broader retail challenge," not the pitch.

**Lesson for Nexus:** Build a **one-line positioning statement** around a shared operational truth. For Nexus, the analog is: *"Every channel thinks it owns the inventory. Nexus gives every team one source of truth."* This is a stronger wedge than "we're an OMS."

### 2.2 Audience Targeting

- **Primary target:** **Qualified Shopify retailers with at least 5 stores.** This is the ICP (ideal customer profile) — big enough to have real multi-location complexity, small enough to have outgrown Shopify's native fulfillment.
- **Goal:** Generate **booth traffic, qualified meetings, and post-event engagement** — not just impressions.
- **Reality check (from Ron Thurston):** Podcast and newsletter audiences **cannot be precisely targeted** to the 5-store Shopify threshold. So HotWax uses broad amplification and lets the *content* filter for the right audience.

**Lesson for Nexus:** Define your ICP precisely (Shopify merchants, 5+ stores, with an ERP like NetSuite) but use **broad content** to attract them — the content itself does the qualification.

### 2.3 Content Amplification (the "Road to NRF" engine)

HotWax's amplification plan spans before, during, and after the event:

| Channel | Use |
|---------|-----|
| **Podcast** | Ron Thurston hosts; HotWax provides source material, he develops in his own voice |
| **LinkedIn** | Ron + HotWax post collaborative content |
| **Newsletter** | HotWax's own newsletter promotes the session |
| **"Road to NRF" materials** | Pre-event teaser content |
| **Student-focused outreach** | Broaden reach to future retail talent |
| **Post-event content** | Reuse the session as evergreen content |

**Key mechanic:** HotWax provides **source material (ideas, quotes, marketing assets)**; the influencer (Ron) creates content in his **independent voice** and makes **reusable versions** HotWax can repost. This is **co-created amplification** — the influencer's credibility + HotWax's substance.

**Lesson for Nexus:** Build a **content engine** where you supply the raw material (data, quotes, problem framing) and let industry voices distribute it. One session becomes a podcast, LinkedIn posts, a newsletter, and evergreen content.

### 2.4 Booth & On-Site Activation

- **Book signing** — Ron signs *Retail Pride* / *Human Pride* at the booth (Sunday 10am–noon) to draw traffic.
- **Multi-day booth presence** — Ron appears at the booth on multiple days.
- **Postcard** — booth number + website QR code to drive follow-up.
- **Direct attendees to the booth** — the session ends with an invitation to continue at the booth.
- **Customer dinner — deprioritized** — "typically expensive and produces lower returns at NRF." Ron explicitly recommended **booth activations over a dinner**.

**Lesson for Nexus:** At any conference, prioritize **booth activations that create a reason to stop** (a signing, a demo, a giveaway) over expensive dinners. Have a **physical takeaway** (postcard/QR) that drives post-event follow-up.

### 2.5 Session Format (the thought-leadership template)

- **Customer perspective** — a real customer story (HotWax–Steve Madden) rather than a product pitch.
- **Limited, high-impact slides** — not a feature dump.
- **~5 minutes of audience Q&A** — engagement, not monologue.
- **Clear invitation to the booth** — the session is a funnel, not an end.
- **Panel format** — Aditya Patel (HotWax) + Ron Thurston (host) + Colleen Waters (Steve Madden) + a store-operations participant. Mixing vendor + customer + industry voice adds credibility.

**Lesson for Nexus:** When you get a speaking slot, use a **customer story + panel format**, keep slides minimal, and always end with a call to continue the conversation. The session is a lead-gen funnel.

## 3. The Partner Channel (Tuihq / Jonathan Sew Hoy)

### 3.1 The Partner Model

**Jonathan Sew Hoy (Tuihq)** is HotWax's most important channel partner. He:
- **Brings prospects** — Malbon, Dollskill, Gallskill, SimKai, RFID prospects.
- **Schedules demos** — coordinates demo timing with both sides.
- **Provides prospect feedback** — "will provide feedback on Malbon after speaking with its operations director."
- **Is building a paid consulting arm** — implementation coordination, project management, order-routing consulting.

**Lesson for Nexus:** Recruit **implementation consultants/partners** who already serve Shopify retailers. They are a direct source of qualified leads and can white-label your implementation. Offer them a referral fee or a paid consulting role.

### 3.2 The Pipeline (from the Tuihq meeting)

| Prospect | Pain / Trigger | HotWax angle |
|----------|---------------|--------------|
| **Malbon** | Under Celigo contract until ~Apr/May; improving inventory accuracy via cycle counts + retail app; **RFID under evaluation** | Replace Celigo; aggressive pricing; RFID-ready |
| **Dollskill** | **Purple Dot contract expiring** within months; urgent replacement before Nov/Dec | Preorder demo: creation, PO linkage, reservation, fulfillment-release, routing, split/hold options |
| **Gallskill** | Complex preorder model (multiple POs, arrival dates, waitlist groups, notifications, cancellations); ~810k orders/yr (68k/mo); Halloween peak; needs pickup-in-store, ship-from-store, dropship | Full omnichannel; variant-level promise dates; PO-linked preorders |
| **SimKai** | Inventory transfers between consignment + store locations | Transfer-order demo in Shopify sandbox with metafields (customer name, return date, purpose) |

**Key trigger pattern:** HotWax wins deals when a **competitor contract expires** (Celigo, Purple Dot). They time their pitch to the contract cycle and position as the replacement.

**Lesson for Nexus:** **Track competitor contract expirations** (Celigo, Purple Dot, Shopify native, other OMS). Time outreach to the renewal window. The demo should explicitly show "here's how we replace what you're paying for."

### 3.3 Pricing (from the Tuihq meeting — concrete numbers)

- **Malbon proposal:** ~**$1,500/mo** during the remaining Celigo term (Rails-equivalent capabilities, **excluding inventory management**), increasing to ~**$3,000–$3,200/mo** afterward.
- **Pricing philosophy:** Jonathan preferred **aggressive pricing over a phased inventory rollout** because Malbon already has an inventory tool. Don't sell what the client already has.
- **Dev work:** ~**$150/hr** SOW (from gorjana meeting).
- **Implementation:** ~$1,000 one-time (from SIMKHAI).

**Lesson for Nexus:** Price **aggressively to displace an incumbent**, then raise after the contract term. Don't bundle features the client already has — sell the gap, not the whole platform. Have a **per-order tier** and a **dev-rate SOW** ready.

## 4. What Nexus Should Copy vs. Beat

### Copy (proven to work)
- ✅ **Problem-led positioning** — "shared scoreboard" style narrative, not feature pitch
- ✅ **Thought-leadership content engine** — one session → podcast, LinkedIn, newsletter, evergreen
- ✅ **Co-created amplification** — supply source material, let industry voices distribute
- ✅ **Conference booth activations** — a reason to stop + QR takeaway; skip expensive dinners
- ✅ **Partner channel** — consultants bring qualified leads
- ✅ **Competitor-contract timing** — pitch at renewal windows
- ✅ **Aggressive displacement pricing** — low to win, raise after term

### Beat (HotWax's weaknesses)
- 🚫 **Reactive implementation** → Nexus: **standard go-live checklist, self-serve onboarding**
- 🚫 **No roll-off process** → Nexus: **clear hypercare → self-sufficiency transition**
- 🚫 **Consulting dependency** → Nexus: **self-serve where possible**
- 🚫 **Narrow targeting** (can't reach 5-store Shopify precisely) → Nexus: **use content + ICP filters to qualify at scale**
- 🚫 **Product-pitch risk at NRF** → Nexus: **always lead with customer outcomes, never feature dumps**

HotWax's marketing is **thought-leadership-led and partner-driven**: name an industry problem, get an influencer to amplify it, and let consultants bring qualified prospects.
